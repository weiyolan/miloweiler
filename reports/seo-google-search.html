SEO report: Google Search & miloweiler.com

Source: Google Search Central. Written 11 Nov 2026. Branch ccr-172d8586-2aemz0.

Can a button make Milo appear in Google more often? Not directly: nothing a visitor clicks raises Milo's position for everyone else. The button that helps most is "Review me on Google". Google reviews strengthen Milo's Google Business Profile, which is what shows up in Google Maps and in local searches like "photographer Antwerp".

Done: a "Review me on Google" button with the Google G icon in the footer (EN/FR/NL). Milo controls it from the Studio, and it stays hidden until he pastes his review link. I also kept the Sanity Studio and the 404 page out of Google.

Tried and dropped: Google's "Preferred sources" button. miloweiler.com isn't listed in Google's tool, so the button led to an empty page (§1).

Biggest real wins still to do (outside the code): create the Google Business Profile, Google Search Console, and links from other sites (see §4).

1. The footer button

"Review me on Google" (what the site has now)

Review me on Google
  • Why reviews: for a local business, reviews are one of the factors Google uses to rank a Business Profile in Maps and local results (see Google's "Tips to improve your local ranking"). It's also the one thing a happy visitor or client can do in 30 seconds that actually helps.
  • Where: in the footer on every page that has one, below the language and theme switches (src/components/Footer2.jsx). Labels: EN "Review me on Google", FR "Évaluez-moi sur Google", NL "Beoordeel mij op Google". It opens Google's review form in a new tab, with no Google script on the site (so no cookies to declare).
  • How Milo turns it on:
    1. Create or claim the Google Business Profile at business.google.com, using the same name, address and phone number as the website.
    2. Once it's verified, search "Milo Weiler Photography" on Google while signed in, then click "Ask for reviews" (sometimes called "Get more reviews") and copy the link. It looks like https://g.page/r/XXXX/review.
    3. In the Studio, go to Business Info → Google → Google review link, paste it, publish, and trigger the Netlify deploy.
    The button stays hidden while that field is empty, so the site never links to a page that doesn't work.
  • Also use the same link in the email Milo sends after delivering photos, and on invoices or a QR code at events. That's where most reviews actually come from.

Why not Google's "Preferred sources" button

  • Per Google's docs, a visitor who picks a site as a "preferred source" sees it more often in Top Stories, AI Mode and AI Overviews, and only for that visitor. Normal web results and Google Images aren't affected.
  • Every version of that button (the JavaScript button, the custom API and the plain link) opens the same Google tool, and miloweiler.com isn't listed in it. Google offers no way to apply. SEO reports say the tool favours sites that publish often, such as news sites, so a portfolio rarely qualifies. So we removed it.

2. Other fix in this change

IssueFix
/studio (the Sanity admin) had the page title "Next.js", the description "Generated by Next.js" and no noindex. Google could index it as a junk result for "Milo Weiler". src/app/studio/layout.js: the title is now "Milo Weiler | Studio". src/app/layout.js: robots: noindex, nofollow (checked: <meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow"> is rendered). I deliberately did not add a robots.txt Disallow, because that would stop Google from reading the noindex.
The 404 page ("Blank Film") is served by the same app-router layout, so it also had the title "Next.js". Its title is now "Page not found | Milo Weiler Photography", with noindex. It still returns HTTP 404.

4. What actually makes Milo appear more often (to-do, mostly outside the code)

  1. Google Search Console (search.google.com/search-console) is the most important step. Verify miloweiler.com (a DNS record at the domain host, or Netlify), submit https://miloweiler.com/sitemap.xml, and use URL Inspection → "Request indexing" after big new projects. It also shows which searches already bring visitors, so you can see what is working.
  2. Google Business Profile (business.google.com). For searches like "photographer Antwerp" or "fotograaf Mortsel", this is the strongest lever: a Maps listing, photos and reviews. Use exactly the same name, address and phone number as the site's structured data (Hof Savelkoul 40, 2640 Mortsel, +32 476 50 62 09).
  3. Reviews. Ask happy clients for a Google review. Reviews raise the Business Profile in local results.
  4. Links from other sites. Google uses links to discover and rank pages. Good sources: film and production credits (IMDb, production company sites), client sites ("photos: Milo Weiler" + a link), local press, photo awards, and Unsplash/Instagram/LinkedIn bios pointing to miloweiler.com.
  5. done Descriptive projects. Google calls helpful content "likely more than any other suggestion" in importance. Checked in Sanity (11 Nov 2026): all 61 projects have a description in EN, FR and NL (106–836 characters), and every photo has EN alt text. Keep doing this for each new project. FR/NL alt text is a small extra.
  6. Be patient. Google says to allow several weeks before judging any change. Re-deploying more often does not speed this up.

  • /studio renders <title>Milo Weiler | Studio</title> and <meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">. The 404 page renders "Page not found | Milo Weiler Photography" with noindex and returns HTTP 404.
